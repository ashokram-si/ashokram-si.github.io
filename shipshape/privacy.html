Shipshape privacy policy

Last updated 23 November 2026

Shipshape is an app for Jira, built by Ashok Ram on Atlassian's Forge platform. It tells you whether a Jira work item is ready to ship. This page explains what it reads and what it keeps.

The short version

Shipshape keeps nothing. It reads what it needs at the moment you look at a work item or ask the Ship readiness agent, shows you the answer, and forgets it. Nothing leaves Atlassian's platform.

What it reads

When you open a work item with the Ship readiness panel, or ask the Ship readiness agent in Rovo about one, Shipshape reads:

  • the work item's description and status, from Jira;
  • the pull requests, deployments and recent comments linked to that work item, from Atlassian's Teamwork Graph;
  • the names of the people who wrote those pull requests and comments, as they appear in Jira.

It reads all of this as you, with your own permissions. If you can't see something in Jira, Shipshape can't see it either.

What it keeps

Nothing. Shipshape has no database and uses no app storage. Each answer is worked out during the request and shown to you, then discarded.

Where it runs

Shipshape runs entirely on Atlassian Forge, inside Atlassian's cloud. It doesn't call any server outside Atlassian, and it doesn't send your data anywhere else. Atlassian's platform may keep standard technical logs of the app running. Shipshape itself doesn't write your data to those logs.

Rovo

When you use the Ship readiness agent, your conversation happens in Rovo, and Atlassian's own terms for Rovo apply to it. Shipshape only supplies the readiness check for the work item you ask about.

Cookies and tracking

Shipshape uses no cookies, analytics or tracking of any kind.

Removing it

A Jira admin can uninstall Shipshape at any time. Since it keeps nothing, there's nothing left to delete afterwards.

Changes

If this ever changes, this page will say so, with a new date at the top.
